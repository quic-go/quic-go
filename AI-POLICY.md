# AI Usage Policy

The quic-go project has strict rules for AI usage:

- **All AI usage in any form must be disclosed.**
  You must state the tool you used (e.g. Claude Code, Cursor, Codex) along with the extent that the
  work was AI-assisted.

- **You must fully understand any code you propose merging into quic-go.**
  Using AI tools to write code is fine. It is your responsibility to understand every line of the
  change and reason through its effects on the codebase.
  **IF YOU DON'T UNDERSTAND THE CODE YOU'RE ASKING US TO MERGE, DO NOT SUBMIT THE PULL REQUEST.**

- **Issues and discussions can use AI assistance but must have a full human-in-the-loop.**
  This means that any prose generated with AI must have been reviewed _and edited_ by a human before
  submission.
  See the [guidance for written submissions](#issues-pull-requests-and-security-disclosures) below.

These rules apply only to outside contributions to quic-go. Maintainers are exempt from these rules
and may use AI tools at their discretion, they've proven themselves trustworthy to apply good
judgment.

## Issues, Pull Requests, and Security Disclosures

quic-go is open source software used in many different projects. As maintainers, we often don't know
how people are using it, and we love hearing about the things people are building with it. When
opening an issue, submitting a pull request, or reporting a security issue:

- **Tell us how you use quic-go.**
  One sentence about your software and what you use the library for is enough.

- **Tell us how the issue affects you.**
  If you're encountering a problem, briefly describe its impact on your use of the library.

- **Write a short description in your own words.**
  Perfect spelling and grammar don't matter to us. We'd rather read three sentences of imperfect
  English than three pages of smooth AI-generated prose. What matters is hearing from a human about
  a problem they're actually encountering.

- **Skip the background we already have.**
  We maintain this codebase; you don't need to explain how the entire system works or research which
  commit introduced a bug. Focus on the details needed to understand and reproduce your issue or
  review your change.

Issues and pull requests that show signs of unsophisticated AI use will be closed without detailed review.

## There are Humans Here

Please remember that quic-go is maintained by humans.

Every discussion, issue, and pull request is read and reviewed by humans (and sometimes machines,
too). It is a boundary point at which people interact with each other and the work done. It is rude
and disrespectful to approach this boundary with low-effort, unqualified work, since it puts the
burden of validation on the maintainer.

In a perfect world, AI would produce high-quality, accurate work every time. But today, that reality
depends on the driver of the AI. And today, most drivers of AI are just not good enough. So, until
either the people get better, the AI gets better, or both, we have to have strict rules to protect
maintainers.

## AI is Welcome Here

quic-go is written with plenty of AI assistance, and we embrace AI tools as a productive tool in
their workflow. As a project, we welcome AI as a tool!

**Our reason for the strict AI policy is not due to an anti-AI stance**, but instead due to the
number of highly unqualified people using AI. It's the people, not the tools, that are the problem.

I include this section to be transparent about the project's usage about AI for people who may
disagree with it, and to address the misconception that this policy is anti-AI in nature.
